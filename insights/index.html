Original Engineering Reference / Optical 01

What does an optical link budget actually tell you?

A link is viable only when the optical losses and reserved operating margin are within the available transmitter-to-receiver power budget, subject to the relevant equipment specifications. Distance alone cannot establish whether a path will work.

Required budget = fiber loss + connector loss + splice loss + reserved marginAll terms must use consistent units, commonly decibels.

For an illustrative 20 km path with assumed attenuation of 0.30 dB/km, two connector interfaces at 0.50 dB each, no modeled splices and a 3.0 dB reserve, the required budget is 10.0 dB: (20 × 0.30) + (2 × 0.50) + 0 + 3.0.

This is a teaching calculation, not a vendor compatibility recommendation. Actual engineering must use the specified wavelength, fiber type, optic transmit power and receive threshold, measured connections, aging reserve, and applicable installation standards.

Method and limitations

Inputs in this example are explicitly assumed, not field measurements. Optical transceivers may also impose maximum receive-power constraints; excessive power and attenuation are different failure modes.

Manufacturer specifications, site measurements, and theoretical calculations answer different engineering questions. A successful optical power-budget estimate does not replace installation testing or commissioning evidence.

Editorial Standard

Technical integrity is the product.

Our engineering references state the assumptions behind each calculation and explain where project-specific measurements are still needed. Where information is incomplete, the uncertainty remains explicit rather than being presented as an operating result.
